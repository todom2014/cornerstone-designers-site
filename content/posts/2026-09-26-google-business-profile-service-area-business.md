If you go to your customers instead of them coming to you, Google treats you as a service-area business, and setting up a **Google Business Profile for a service-area business** works a little differently from a shop's. Roofers, cleaners, lawn care crews, mobile pet groomers and plenty of home-based businesses across South Carolina fall into this group. Get the setup right and your business can appear on Google Maps and in the map results people see when they search for a service nearby, without putting your home address on the internet.

Everything below comes from Google's own Business Profile help pages, which are linked at the end.

## What counts as a service-area business?

Google defines it as "a business that visits or delivers to customers directly but doesn't serve customers at their business address." If customers never come to your address, you're one.

Some businesses are both: a bakery that also delivers, or a repair shop that also makes house calls. Google calls these hybrid businesses, and its instruction for them is to "enter both your address and service area."

To be eligible for a profile at all, your business needs either a location customers can visit or to travel to customers where they are.

## How do you create a Google Business Profile for a service-area business?

1. Go to **business.google.com/add** and sign in with the Google account you want to own the profile. Use a business account you'll keep, not a personal one you might lose access to.
2. Click **Add your business to Google** and enter your business name exactly as customers know it.
3. Choose your **primary category** (more on this below).
4. When Google asks about a location customers can visit, answer based on whether customers actually come to you. If they don't, you'll add the areas you serve instead.
5. Add your phone number and website.
6. Choose a verification option and follow the steps.

It takes about fifteen minutes. Verification can take longer; see below.

## Which address should you use, and will it be public?

Use the real address your business operates from, which for many service businesses is home. Google's guidance is direct: "If you don't serve customers at your business address, remove your address from your Business Profile." A hidden address stays private, but Google keeps it on file, and your service area is measured from where your business is based.

What you can't use:

- **A P.O. box.** Google's guidelines say P.O. boxes and "mailboxes located at remote locations aren't acceptable."
- **A virtual office**, unless it has signage, is staffed by your own people, and receives customers during business hours.

A virtual mailbox or rented mailbox is fine for receiving mail and for the address you print in emails. It just isn't allowed as your Business Profile address.

## How do you choose your service areas?

List the cities, towns or ZIP codes you actually travel to. Google's limits:

- **Up to 20 service areas.**
- The overall area "shouldn't be more than about 2 hours of driving time from where your business is based."

Pick the places you really serve, not every town you'd hypothetically drive to. To change them later, open your profile, choose **Edit profile**, then **Location**, then **Edit** next to **Service area**. Google says approved changes can take up to 48 hours to appear.

One profile covers your whole area. Google's guidelines call for one profile per business location with a designated service area, so don't create extra profiles for each town you visit.

## What should you name the business, and which category should you pick?

**Name:** use your real-world business name, exactly as it appears on your truck, invoices and signage. Google's guidelines rule out adding marketing taglines, phone numbers, web addresses or service keywords to the name. You'll see competitors named things like "Smith Roofing Best Roofer Columbia SC." Whitespark's 2026 survey of local search experts does rank keywords in the business name among the top map-ranking factors, which is why people do it. It still breaks Google's rules, and it isn't worth putting your profile at risk.

**Category:** this matters more than almost anything else you'll choose. Google says "the categories you select affect your local ranking," and the same Whitespark survey put the **primary category** at the very top of its map-ranking factors for 2026.

- Choose the most specific category that fits. Google's own example: "instead of 'Salon,' choose 'Nail salon.'"
- Add a few additional categories only for real parts of your business. Google says not to pick "a category for every product or service."
- You can't invent a category. If yours isn't listed, pick the closest general one.

## How does verification work?

Google has to confirm you run the business before your profile goes public. The options include phone or text, email, a live video call, and a postcard by mail, and **Google chooses which ones you're offered**. You can't pick.

If you're offered a video call, Google asks you to show:

- where you are, such as a street sign or nearby businesses
- your business or equipment, such as branded tools, a work vehicle or marketing materials
- proof you manage the business, such as access to things only the owner or staff would have

Google says verification reviews "take up to 5 business days," occasionally longer. Have your equipment and paperwork ready before you start a video call so you only have to do it once.

## What else should you fill in?

A complete profile gives customers what they need to call you. Fill in:

- **Phone number:** a number that reaches your business directly. A separate business line keeps your cell number private.
- **Website:** link to your homepage, or to the page for your main service.
- **Hours:** Whitespark's 2026 survey lists being open at the time of the search as a top-five map factor, so keep your hours accurate, including holidays.
- **Services:** in some categories, service businesses among them, you can list each service and add a price and description.
- **Description:** up to 750 characters on what you do and what makes you different. Google asks you not to include links or promotional offers here.
- **Photos:** real photos of your work, your vehicle and your team.
- **Posts:** short updates, offers or event announcements that show on Search and Maps. Google archives posts after six months unless you set a date range, so add a new one now and then.

## What actually helps you show up on the map?

Nobody outside Google knows exactly how local rankings work, and nobody can promise you a spot. Whitespark's 2026 survey is the best-known snapshot of expert opinion. Its top map factors were your primary category, how close you are to the person searching, keywords in the business name, having a physical address in the city being searched, and being open at the time of the search. Review quantity and how recent your reviews are also made the top ten.

Some of those you control (category, hours, reviews) and some you don't (where the customer is standing). For the regular search results under the map, the survey's top factor was a **dedicated page on your website for each service**. That's where your website does work your profile can't. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use my home address for my Business Profile?

Yes. Enter it, then remove it from public view. Customers won't see it, but Google needs it to know where you're based.

### Can I use a P.O. box or a virtual mailbox?

No. Google's guidelines don't accept P.O. boxes or mailboxes at remote locations as a Business Profile address.

### Can I make a separate profile for each town I serve?

No. A service-area business gets one profile per location, with its service areas listed on it. Extra profiles for towns where you have no location go against Google's guidelines.

### How long until my profile shows up on Google?

Once you finish verification, Google says reviews take up to five business days. Later changes to your service area can take up to 48 hours to appear.
